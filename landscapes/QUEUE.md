## Reported from the iPhone Duo

The user's reports, newest first, until each is fixed or answered.

From the list of 2026-11-23, still open:

- **Switching tabs flashes the pictures for a frame.** They should already be decoded and shown when the tab appears.
- **Search, folded: tapping the field shifts the screen's contents down a little.**
- **Search: the large X should not clear the field's text.** The template's `cancel=keepQuery` calls `preventDefault()`, and the app keeps `query`; but UIKit empties its bar as the close begins, and Exact writes the text back only in `didDismissSearchController`, after the closing animation, so the field shows empty while it closes (`HeaderSearchIOS.swift`).
- **Search, folded, then a landscape, then unfolding, does not land on that landscape.** Folded and unfolded should be structurally the same screen and controller hierarchy, built from UIKit's own controllers, so a fold or an unfold keeps where the user is. On the Duo simulator at `4ecb7759be` the unfold lands on the landscape, with the query kept in the sidebar; the structure is still not UIKit's: the results are a route pushed on the stack (`/search`), where UIKit shows a search's results in its `UISearchController`'s results controller over the screen that owns the search (LLP 1134 records that a header search has none in Exact).
- **Swiping back from a landscape to the search results pops the search bar up over the landscape.** The bar belongs to the results' screen and should go back with it. Reproduced on the folded Duo simulator: filmed, the results' search field and X are across the bottom over the landscape from the swipe's first frames. The same structure as above: the results are a pushed route that carries the search bar.
- **The hero's press should ease its darkening on the platform's timing.** Exact times a press as LLP 1061 D7 says: the web and painted hosts and the decision have landed (expo/exact#1451, expo/exact#1471, expo/exact-llp#257), and the Apple hosts are in a fix round (`ide/press-transition`). Then compare the hero with the SwiftUI sample on the phone; its words no longer darken.
- **Folded, the main screen's Back menu shows an empty screen name.** The route's own `head title` should name it there, with a compile warning for a blank heading and no new syntax (LLP 1075.003 §9.10, a draft). The draft waits on how UIKit's Back menu names an untitled screen, which the probe on AppDrop shows on the phone.

- **The agent cannot reopen a search that a close collapsed back into its toolbar button** (Exact): `tap` aims at a collapsed search through the button centred on its field, and once closed the field leaves the window.

From the same list, fixed on `landscapes/map-resize`, to confirm on the next build: the strips' insets and scroll offsets at a partial fold, the sidebar's rows appearing in place at launch, the purple checkmarks, the hero no longer peekable (expo/exact#1437 upstream), a peeked card following its strip's scroll, the scroll offsets kept through backgrounding, the Map full screen when folded and under the open sidebar, the Maps logo with Info open at a partial fold, the empty collection's Select button, the freeze when searching at a partial fold, and the list rows' chevrons. Earlier reports (build 11, AppDrop, 2026-10-31, runs the Duo series at `8649d0f1a5`; `9a5d74eb5f` since the series was rewritten):

- **Info opened closed, then the Duo unfolded, stays a large sheet; dismissed, the page's bar buttons stop working** (2026-11-07, build 15). Not reproduced on the Duo simulator: closed, a landmark's or a collection's landmark's Info is a sheet, and unfolding (at once, swept over two seconds, twice over, and while the sheet was still presenting) moves it to the inspector's column; dismissed by a swipe, Favorite and Info work. The column takes the route in `NavigationHost.sync` (`toColumn`, `placeInspector`), which defers while the split expands or a sheet moves and retries when the presentation ends or the split reports its change. A trace from the phone (a development build's Save Trace) would show which of those the unfold missed.
- **The sidebar's bar showed Collections' + and its title on the Map page** (2026-11-07, build 15, after unfolding at some point). Not reproduced yet: closed, Collections, then the list and Map, then unfolding, shows the sidebar's own bar.
- **Tapping a map marker is slow** (2026-11-07; the sample too). Both select a marker into MapKit's place card (`mapItemDetailSelectionAccessory()`, `MKSelectionAccessory.mapItemDetail`). Filmed on the open Duo simulator, the marker grows the frame after the tap and the card rises at once, empty with a spinner, and fills when MapKit has the place's details from Apple's servers: 1.5 s here, 0.7 s in the sample, as the network answered.

- **A peeked card is cut off at its top and bottom** (2026-11-02, the main screen's strips). Measured on the open Duo simulator with real touches: build 11 lifts a picture of the card targeted at its superview, the strip's `UIScrollView`, exactly as tall as the cards, and UIKit grows the picture inside it, so mid-lift the card is 7% wider and still the strip's 140 points tall, its shadow cut at the strip's edges. With ccheever/exact2 #1310, which lifts the card itself, the card stays whole and leaves the strip as it grows; only the shadow UIKit draws while the press highlights, before the lift, ends at the strip's edges for a few frames. That is UIKit's own behavior: a plain UIKit app's horizontal `UICollectionView` of 140-point cells, and plain views in a 140-point `UIScrollView` with UIKit's default preview, highlight the same way. Fixed by #1310 on the series, with its later fixes in build 13: the card's shadow whole, no flashing rectangle; the user on the phone (2026-11-02): "New landscapes has better peek".
- **A released peek leaves the card blank for about a second** (2026-11-02). Measured: build 11 hides the card (`alpha` 0) while its picture is lifted and shows it again only when UIKit's dismissal completes; UIKit shrinks and fades the picture instead of returning it, so the card's place is white for 23 frames (0.77 s on a loaded Mac) and the card snaps back. With #1310 the host hides nothing and UIKit returns the card itself: no white frame.
- **The featured landmark's touch feedback** (2026-10-29, 2026-10-31): the sample's wash covers the hero's full width, and it fades in and out. The whole hero and its mirror now fade together; its depth and its release still differ (below).
- **The badges are less bright than SwiftUI's** (2026-10-29), which wants an answer why. Measured on the simulator, which is SDR: the same system colours as the sample's source, and Exact's fill is if anything a little brighter, so an HDR difference can only be seen on the phone. Their menu now dissolves out in place (`-exact-exit-animation`). What differs from the sample: its bloom and dissolve blur the content, which is SwiftUI's own (UIKit offers no public form of it), and its button's symbol cross-fades where Exact's changes at once. `-exact-symbol-effect: replace` is refused on a native button's `image`; UIKit's form is `UIButton.Configuration.symbolContentTransition`.
- **Focusing a new collection's title scrolls too far** (2026-10-31; below).
- **The right sidebar is not draggable** (2026-10-31). Measured: UIKit, the sample and Landscapes resize the inspector from its leading edge alike, and none opens or closes it with a drag. Waiting on which drag the user tried.
- **From the split work** (2026-10-30): the main content should follow the sidebar's curve, and the segmented control sometimes loses its background.
- **While search is focused, a pushed page's bar buttons go, Back included** (2026-11-08). On the open Duo simulator with nothing typed, the sample keeps the landmark page's Back, Share and Favorite (the rest in ⋯ above the keyboard); Exact shows none. The sidebar's own button goes in both, which is UIKit's. Not caused by the search results: it shows with the field empty.
- **Focusing search moves less than the sample** (2026-11-06). The sample's page scrolls up as the bar goes and its badge button rides above the keyboard; here the page stays and the button stays behind the keyboard.
